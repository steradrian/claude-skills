# Film types: structure and pacing

Every film needs one idea with a turn in it. Structure is how that turn gets delivered.

## Product reveal (reference: `photoreal` starter)

Tension, then the product, then proof, then the name.

| Beat | Share of runtime | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Glimpses | 15 to 25% | Macro fragments in darkness, cut on sound. Mystery, craft, no full view. |
| The line | 5 to 10% | One sentence on black that sets up the turn. |
| The hit | 10 to 15% | Sound hit and light arrive together. The first full view, and it's a move, not a pose. |
| Proof | 25 to 35% | The product doing the one thing it does. For software, push into the screen. |
| Resolve | 15 to 20% | Pull back, name, line, call to action. Hold long enough to read twice. |

Lengths: 15 s is hit, proof and name only. 30 s adds two glimpses and the line. 45 to 60 s is the full structure.

## Brand or promo film (reference: `stylized` starter)

Personality first. Energy that builds, a surprise, a clean button.

| Beat | Share | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | first 1 to 1.5 s | Something moves or lands. Never open on a logo. |
| Character | 20 to 30% | The product has a personality in how it moves: squash, bounce, a hop. |
| Offer | 30 to 40% | What it gives you, shown as objects or UI, three items at most. |
| Surprise | 10 to 15% | One unexpected beat: a spin, a reversal, a camera move. |
| Button | 15 to 20% | Name and line. Everything else tidies away first. |

## UI walkthrough or explainer (reference: `motion2d` starter)

Problem, turn, three proofs, lockup.

| Beat | Share | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | 15 to 20% | Kinetic type names a pain the viewer recognises. Show it as a visual metaphor too (the "tabs" in the starter). |
| Turn | 10 to 15% | A graphic transition (wipe, morph, zoom) flips to the promise. |
| Proof x3 | 45 to 55% | Three features, each a caption plus the real interaction: tap, type, result. One idea per beat. |
| Lockup | 15 to 20% | Mark, name, line, call to action. |

Keep UI copy real and specific. "Plan a tiny book launch" sells; "Lorem ipsum" or "Feature one" kills it.

## Pacing rules for every type

- **The first 1.5 s decides whether a vertical reel gets watched.** Motion or a question in frame one.
- **A line of on-screen text needs about 0.25 s per word plus 0.6 s to settle before it can leave.**
- **Cuts and transitions land on sound cues.** Build the cue list with the shot list.
- **Vary energy.** Stillness before a hit makes the hit. Three beats at the same speed read as a slideshow.
- **Give the end card at least 3 s after the last element lands.**

## Formats

- **9:16** (Reels, TikTok, Shorts): the product upright, text in the middle 70% of the height.
- **16:9** (web, YouTube): wider lenses feel cheap, so keep them long and use the width for motion.
- **1:1 and 4:5** (feeds): tighter framing, bigger type.

Whatever the format, keep text inside the safe area: 5% from the sides, 4% from the top and bottom. The review flags anything outside it.
