# Concepting

A concept is a *reason* the ad exists: one idea, told with an arc, that could only belong to this
brand. Styles make it look good; the concept makes it worth watching.

## Start from a product truth

Before writing concepts, write down 3 to 5 true things about the product (from the brief). Look
for one that is surprising, visual, or contains a tension:
- OFFCUT: "made from factory scraps" → tension between *waste* and *desirable*.
- A budgeting app: "most people check their balance before buying coffee" → a tiny daily anxiety.
- A night market: "it only exists 6 hours a week" → scarcity.

Then pick the style that *embodies* that truth where possible (scraps → collage; precision tool →
Swiss grid; nightlife → neon). When the style and the truth rhyme, the ad feels designed rather
than decorated.

## Arc archetypes

Use three *different* archetypes for the three concepts so the user has a real choice.

1. **The reframe.** Show how the world sees it, strike it out, replace it. ("They called it WASTE."
   → "We called it OFFCUT 01.") Strong hook, needs a sharp two-line copy pair.
2. **The assembly.** Pieces fly in and build the product/logo. Great when the product is literally
   made of parts, ingredients or features.
3. **The countdown / list.** 3 reasons, 5 ingredients, 1 date. Rhythmic, easy to cut to music.
   Risk: feels like a listicle; each item needs its own visual idea.
4. **Before / after.** Chaos → order, grey → colour, messy desk → one app. Works muted. Make the
   switch a single, satisfying moment.
5. **The reveal.** Tease with extreme close-ups, textures, fragments; pull back to reveal the
   product at the end. Good for premium; needs a hero worth revealing.
6. **The manifesto.** Big type, one line per beat, building to a belief. For brands selling an
   attitude. Risk: pompous; keep lines short and concrete.
7. **The dare / challenge.** Direct address: "Bet you can't...", "Try not to...". Playful, social.
8. **A day in 15 seconds.** Montage of moments across a day, each with the product. Needs variety
   of visuals; hardest in pure code without photos.

## Hooks (first 1.5 seconds)

The first frame must already be interesting (not a fade from black to an empty screen). Options:
a provocative line, an extreme close-up, motion already in progress, a sound-less "slam", a
question. Put the brand name early only if brand recall is the goal.

## Concept card format

```
### A · <Concept name>
Hook: "<the first line or image>"
Arc: 1) ... 2) ... 3) ... 4) ... (4 to 6 beats, one line each)
End card: <logo + line + CTA>
Why it fits: <the product truth it dramatises, 1 to 2 lines>
Style it wants: <pack>, <one-line twist>
Risk: <the honest weak point>
```

After the three cards, pick one (SKILL.md Phase 2): the concept that dramatises the truest product
truth with the strongest first 1.5 seconds wins over the prettiest. A merge is allowed when one
card's hook and another's ending are clearly stronger together; write the merged card and say so.
Only when the user chose "I choose from three" do you present the cards and ask.

## Copy rules

- Short. On-screen lines of 2 to 5 words. Read time is about 3 words per second, plus 0.5s.
- Concrete nouns beat adjectives ("forty kilos of canvas" beats "sustainable materials").
- The end line should work alone as a caption.
- Sentence case unless the style pack says otherwise; all-caps only for display type.
